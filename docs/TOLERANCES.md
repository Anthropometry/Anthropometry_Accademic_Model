# Tape-measurement tolerances (for the report and the tailor conversation)

**Source:** 2010 Anthropometric Survey of U.S. Marine Corps Personnel (MC-ANSUR), Methods and Summary Statistics,
Chapter VIII, Table 27 (allowable errors) and Tables 28–34 (observer error), pages 347–352. Same team, measurer
handbook and allowable errors as ANSUR II (Gordon et al., 2014). Pages supplied by Ryan, 25 Nov 2026.

**What the numbers mean**
- **Observer error (MAD):** each re-measured participant was measured twice, by two different trained
  measurers; MAD is the average absolute difference between their two readings.
- **Allowable error:** the limit set before the survey; if a station's weekly MAD went above it, the
  supervisor retrained the measurers. In that survey, every MAD stayed below its allowable error.
- So these are the limits **trained professionals** are held to when measuring the same body twice. A model
  judged against one tape reading also carries that tape's own error; it cannot be fairly held to less.

**Reading it next to our ±1.5 cm target:** trained measurers are allowed 3–14 mm depending on the
measurement; the largest allowances are the big soft girths (chest 14, waist 12, hip 12 mm), the same ones
where the photo model is weakest. Per-measurement tolerances for the final report should be set from this
chart, the tailors' answers and, once collected, the repeat readings in Ryan's own app data.
